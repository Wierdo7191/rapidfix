How to Troubleshoot a Dead TV

6 min read By RapidFix Kenya Technical Team August 18, 2026

You press the power button, tap the remote, and nothing happens. No picture, no sound, no LED indicator light. Your TV is completely dead. Before you assume the worst and start shopping for a replacement, know this: most dead TVs are suffering from power supply issues, not total panel failure. With the right troubleshooting steps, you can often pinpoint the problem and get a clear repair quote before spending a single shilling on a new set.

In this step-by-step guide, we walk through the first checks for a TV that will not turn on, explain common power-related faults, and show you when professional TV repair makes financial sense.

Step 1: Check the Power Source

It sounds obvious, but power issues are the most common cause of a dead TV. Start here before opening any panels:

  1. Verify the power cable is firmly connected at both the wall and the TV
  2. Test the wall socket with another device, such as a phone charger or lamp
  3. Inspect the power cable for visible damage, fraying, or bent prongs
  4. Try a different power cable if your TV model allows it
  5. Reset the TV by unplugging it for 60 seconds, then plugging it back in

Step 2: Test the Remote and Physical Buttons

Sometimes the TV is on, but you cannot tell because there is no picture. Rule out remote failure:

  • Replace remote batteries with fresh ones
  • Look closely at the TV for a faint LED indicator
  • Press the physical power button on the TV panel for three seconds
  • Try a universal remote if you have one available

Step 3: Inspect for Power Surge Damage

Nairobi experiences frequent voltage fluctuations and blackouts. A power surge can fry the TV's power supply board instantly. Signs of surge damage include a burnt smell near the power port, a tripped surge protector, or failure that began immediately after a power outage.

Common Causes of a TV Not Turning On

1. Failed Power Supply Board (PSU)

The power supply board converts AC electricity from your wall into DC power for the TV's internal components. Capacitors on this board are vulnerable to voltage spikes. When they fail, the TV receives no power at all. This is the most common cause of a TV power supply issue in Kenya.

2. Blown Fuse

Many TVs have a glass fuse on the power supply board. If the fuse is blown, the TV will not power on. A technician can replace a fuse in minutes, but the underlying cause of the surge must also be addressed.

3. Faulty Main Board

If the power supply is working but the TV still does not start, the main board may have failed. This board processes signals from inputs and controls the display. Symptoms include no picture but a working backlight, or repeated boot loops.
